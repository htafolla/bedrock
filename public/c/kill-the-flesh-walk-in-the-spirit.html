The standard

Core mission

Kill the acts of the flesh. Walk in the Spirit. This is the path.

(Galatians 5:16, 5:24-25)

Daily standards

1. Thought capture

The moment a destructive thought appears — name-calling, suspicion, rage, control, worst-case spirals, fear — take it captive.

  • Stop.
  • Name the thought.
  • Refuse to feed it.
  • Submit it to the obedience of Christ.

Prayer: “No. I release this to You, Lord.”

(2 Corinthians 10:5, Colossians 3:2)

2. Combat fear

Fight fear with the weapons of the Spirit: power, love, and a sound mind.

When fear rises:

  • Name the fear.
  • Refuse to agree with it.
  • Hand the outcome to God.

Prayer: “God has not given me a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and a sound mind. I hand this to You.”

(2 Timothy 1:7, Ephesians 6:16, Philippians 4:6-7)

3. Refuse the acts of the flesh

No name-calling. No yelling or rage. No control or monitoring. No revenge. No settled bitterness. No using suspicion as permission to return to the flesh.

When the flesh rises:

  • Name the act.
  • Refuse it.
  • Stop and return to obedience.

Prayer: “I refuse this. I will walk in Love.”

(Galatians 5:19-21, Ephesians 4:29, 4:31)

4. Forgiveness

Forgiveness is how you refuse to keep score and stay free from bitterness.

  • Release the debt.
  • Refuse to rehearse the offense.
  • Hand the person and the injustice to God.

Prayer: “I forgive. I release this to You, Lord.”

(Matthew 6:14-15, Ephesians 4:32, Colossians 3:13)

5. Self-control

Refuse self-gratification.

Guard eyes, thoughts, and body.

Avoid emotional and physical affairs or attachments.

Respect boundaries. Do not push for intimacy or emotional processing.

When the urge rises:

  • Stop.
  • Do not pursue self-gratification.
  • Submit the thought or action to Christ.

Prayer: “My body is the temple of the Holy Spirit. I choose self-control. Strengthen me.”

(1 Corinthians 6:19-20, Galatians 5:22-23, 1 Corinthians 6:18)

6. Presence and independent self

You are a whole person under God. Your identity, emotional regulation, and worth do not come from the other person.

Love without disappearing, over-functioning, or managing another person’s emotional state.

When the pull to disappear rises:

  • No over-functioning or managing their emotions.
  • Focus on the people and work God has put in front of you.
  • Your emotional state is not dependent on them.

Prayer: “Lord, this is where You have me. Help me be present where I am.”

(Colossians 3:23, Galatians 6:4-5, Psalm 16:8, Philippians 2:3-4)

7. Honest assessment

You may not fully trust the other person. Do not pretend otherwise.

When distrust rises:

  • Admit it honestly.
  • Do not overcommit or blindly give trust.
  • Do not use the lack of trust as permission for the flesh.

Prayer: “Lord, I do not trust them. Give me peace.”

(Proverbs 4:23, Proverbs 12:22, Ephesians 4:15)

8. Contact and boundaries

Default to low or no contact when the relationship is unstable.

Keep all contact logistical and unemotional.

Do not message to regulate their feelings, reduce your own anxiety, process the relationship, or seek reassurance.

When contact happens:

  • Keep it short and clear.
  • Stay on logistics only (kids, pets, house, money, schedules).
  • Do not bring up the relationship, feelings, forgiveness, or the future, or push for emotional connection unless they open it.
  • If they message, pause, regulate, then respond only to what is necessary.

When the urge to reach out rises:

  • Pause.
  • Ask: Am I trying to regulate them or myself?
  • If it is not logistical and necessary, do not send it.

Prayer: “I will not use contact to manage pain. Give me restraint.”

(Matthew 5:37, Ecclesiastes 3:1)

9. Return to your side of the street

Your side of the street is the only ground you control: thoughts, mouth, actions, obedience, standards.

Their replies, timing, tone, location, and plans are their side.

When you drift:

  • Notice the drift.
  • Stop reading them or building narratives.
  • Return to your side of the street.

Prayer: “Lord, You know what they are doing and what they are thinking. I give them to You.”

(1 Thessalonians 4:11, Galatians 6:4-5, Matthew 7:3)

10. Recurring external pressure

Past patterns and third parties will reappear.

When they do:

  • Notice the pull to monitor, perform, or compete.
  • Do not increase contact or surveillance.
  • Stay on your side of the street.
  • Hand the situation to God.

Prayer: “Lord, protect my spouse and return their heart to me. I will not compete.”

(Romans 12:17-19)

11. Operate from strength, not need

Do not initiate from anxiety or the need to be reassured.

Do not measure your worth by their availability or tone.

When pain or need rises:

  • Stop initiating from anxiety.
  • Bring the raw sentence to the Lord. Do not clean it up.
  • Measure yourself by obedience, not their response.

Prayer: “My emotional state is mine to manage. It is not dependent on them.”

(Psalm 46:10, Exodus 14:14, 1 Peter 5:7)

12. Trust over understanding

Trust in the Lord with all your heart. Lean not on your own understanding.

He is with you.

When questions and mixed signals rise:

  • Refuse the demand to figure it all out.
  • Hand the unanswered questions, mixed signals, and future to Him.
  • Return to your side of the street.

Prayer: “Lord, You know the plans You have for me. Help me trust You.”

(Proverbs 3:5-6, Proverbs 16:9, Jeremiah 29:11)

For men: strength, courage, and leadership

A man is called to lead himself first.

(1 Corinthians 16:13)

Emotional self-responsibility

No one else is responsible for managing or stabilizing your emotional state.

Vulnerability that requires another person to regulate you is not strength.

True strength feels the full weight of loss or desire and still governs mouth, actions, and decisions.

(Proverbs 25:28)

Courage

Courage is not the absence of pain.

It is staying steady when the outcome is uncertain and refusing to use pressure or emotional need to force closeness.

(Joshua 1:9)

Leadership in practice

Lead by governing yourself, not by managing her.

Consistency under pressure is more persuasive than repeated claims of change.

A man who carries his own emotional load becomes safer to be around.

(Ephesians 5:25, Colossians 3:19)

Strength that serves

Strength is the capacity to remain kind, clear, and boundaried at the same time.

It refuses both passive waiting and aggressive pursuit.

(Matthew 23:11, 1 Corinthians 16:13-14)

Success measure

Not whether the other person returns or chooses you.

Success is staying in the Spirit and refusing the flesh — one decision at a time.

(Galatians 6:9, James 1:12)

Do better. Be better. Trust God.

Under fire

  • Thought capture: stop, name it, refuse to feed it, submit it to Christ. “No. I release this to You, Lord.”
  • Return to your side of the street — thoughts, mouth, actions, obedience, standards. Their replies and plans are their side. When you drift into reading them: stop.
  • Success is not whether they return or choose you. Success is staying in the Spirit and refusing the flesh — one decision at a time.

Prayer

Father, I release the flesh and the loop. By Your Spirit I take every thought captive. I hand fear, contact, and outcomes to You. I return to my side of the street. Amen.
